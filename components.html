Build What You Have

Kitbashing

Secundus doesn't care where your models came from. The game was built around the assumption that you will cut, pin, glue, and improvise. A barbarian is whatever you made them out of. A threat is whatever looks like it wants to kill something.

Barbarians

Any humanoid figure works. Scale is a suggestion. Secundus plays with 28mm but has no objection to 15mm, 32mm, or whatever you have in the box. The model should be readable at arm's length — you should be able to tell at a glance whether it's holding a ranged weapon or a melee weapon. That's the only practical constraint.

Equipment

Equipment grants skills. A model with a rifle has Marksman. A model with a maul has Brawler. If you want your barbarian to have a skill, build or convert the model to show it. A backpack for Survivor. A cloak for Camouflage. A staff for Weird. The model is the character sheet.

Threats

Folk can be anything humanoid — cultists, scavengers, corrupted soldiers. Beasts can be anything feral — mutants, hounds, overgrown vermin. Monsters can be anything large or horrific. The roll tells you what it is. The model tells you what it looks like. Both matter.

Terrain & Sites

Sites are marked with tokens, objective markers, or any small object that reads as "this place matters." Ruins, rubble piles, barrels, craters. The table should feel like somewhere the world gave up on. Scatter terrain dense enough to create decision points. Open ground is a death sentence.
